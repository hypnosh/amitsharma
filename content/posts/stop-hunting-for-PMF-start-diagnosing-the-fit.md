As a founder, how often have you said to yourself:

**"We need to find product-market fit."**

Fair enough.

But often, that isn't really the problem.

The harder problem is figuring out **what exactly needs to fit what.**

A product can fail to find traction for very different reasons:

- The product is technically possible, but nobody really needs it.
- People need it, but not enough to pay for it.
- People will pay, but the economics don't work.
- There is demand, but the founder has no particular advantage in serving it.

These are very different problems.

Yet founders often respond to all of them in the same way:

**Build more.**

Add features.

Change the UI.

Try another channel.

Run more ads.

And then wonder why nothing moves.

I think of PMF less as a destination and more as the intersection of four things:

**Can we build it?  
Does someone genuinely need it?  
Will they pay for it?  
And why are we particularly well placed to solve it?**

If those four aren't reasonably aligned, more execution can actually make things worse.

You end up getting very good at building something that shouldn't exist.

**Before asking "How do we get PMF?"**

Ask:

**"What exactly are we trying to make fit?"**

That's usually the more useful question.
