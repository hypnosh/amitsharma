Talks for people building products in the real world

01 · Why Founders Overbuild Before They Overlearn

Capable founders often respond to uncertainty by building more. This session examines how to tell development activity from real learning about what customers need and will pay for.

Audience takeaways:

  • Identify the riskiest customer and business assumptions behind a product idea.
  • Separate evidence of customer learning from evidence that a team has shipped.
  • Choose a useful next validation step before committing to more build work.

02 · Product Decisions When Everything Seems Important

For early-stage founders, product leaders and operators balancing competing demands with limited time and resources. Explore prioritisation, stakeholder pressure and execution bottlenecks, and the discipline required to make consequential choices.

Audience takeaways:

  • Make the trade-offs behind a priority explicit.
  • Distinguish a genuine constraint from a queue of urgent requests.
  • Decide what to defer, test or stop when capacity is tight.

03 · The Product-Market Fit Equation for Domain-Heavy Products

Drawing on the PMF Primer, this practical session explores how technology capability, audience need, willingness to pay and founder expertise interact. The focus is on assumptions, evidence and what to validate next.

Audience takeaways:

  • Map the key assumptions across customer, problem, solution and business model.
  • Look for evidence that audience need connects to willingness to pay.
  • Identify the next test that could change a product decision.

04 · When Motion Stops Feeling Like Progress

For founders and product teams whose roadmaps are busy but outcomes feel stalled. Explore how to distinguish activity from progress, diagnose where momentum is breaking down, and identify the constraint worth addressing first.

Audience takeaways:

  • Connect activity and delivery measures to customer or business outcomes.
  • Trace a stalled result to a specific assumption, handoff or bottleneck.
  • Select a focused next action instead of adding more work to the roadmap.

These experiences inform how I approach today’s early-stage product questions; they are context for the work, not the whole story.
